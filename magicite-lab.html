Roster — pick 5, one main

Mixed models one loadout shared by a MAG attacker and an MND attacker, scoring the weighted mean of their two multipliers. Both Magic Boon and Mind Boon become offensive there, competing for the same slots, and the weight decides which wins.

Magic damage reads MAG for Black Magic and Summon, but MND for White Magic — SUB_CALC_TYPE.MAGIC picks the stat while the curve, attenuation point and ceiling stay identical. In White mode the Mind Boon becomes your offensive Boon (and still boosts healing), the Magic Boon drops out, and Crusader's 30% MND drain hits your damage directly.

The Ultra Gigas (Antlion / Sand Worm / Mimic Queen / Cooler Mammoth) carries an innate Empower 28 but has no Diversion and no seal slots — so it trades a seal and a Diversion for the top of the element pool. Only the one matching your element is worth fielding, which is why there is a single generic entry.

Lock pins a row at its current count: Optimise fills only the unlocked slots around it, and Clear leaves locked rows alone. Lock one Blade Ward and one Spell Ward, then optimise, and the remaining eight slots are solved for damage. Max is how many copies you can actually field. The Lv 28 Empower exists for Fire, Earth, Lightning and Water only, one apiece — set it to 0 on an element you do not have one for. Fielding the Ultra Gigas itself supplies that 28 innately, so this row is disabled while it is in the roster. Both Empower rows feed the same pool, and T() sorts descending, so the Lv 28 always occupies the top slot.

Marginal value is the change in damage-per-time from adding one more copy, given everything else currently selected. Defensive picks (orange) are scored against damage taken, not damage dealt. Blade Ward and Spell Ward gate on the incoming attack type, so both are available to any team — pick whichever matches what the boss deals. Dampen is elemental and cuts either.

All arithmetic mirrors battle.js: pooling via calculateEffectValueOfTalismanMateria (sorted descending, each successive value halved, then ceiled); the soft-capped base term from DamageCalculator; the ATB bar as max(floor(4500 − SPD/0.15), 1) ms, advanced by atbTimeFactor while the cast bar advanced separately by castTimeFactor, which is the product of the status factor (Quick Cast 200, High Quick Cast 300) and Fast Act; stat grants as floor(total_params × T/100) with total_params = main + Σ floor(sub/4).

There is deliberately no enemy-DEF control. Every figure is a ratio of two damages taken against the same target, and DEF enters both through the same min((stat/def)^0.5, 1000) term, so it cancels — and the cap never binds at realistic stats. It would change the absolute damage, but not any number shown here.

Every figure here is the magicite contribution — damage with this loadout divided by damage with no magicite equipped. That includes the crit term, which counts only the Deadly and Precise Strikes the magicite supply, not the base 150% crit factor or your external buffs. It is therefore fair to compare the physical and magic numbers to each other, but neither is an absolute DPS figure, and a physical and a magic character with the same number are not dealing the same damage.

Boons are applied at battle start as setBoost(param, amount, {isBoostAmount: true}). In ActorParamHolder percentage boosts multiply the base stat and flat amounts are summed on afterwards — s = floor(base × rate) + flat — so a Boon never benefits from your in-battle ATK buffs. Past the freeze point the base damage term is frozen and the only remaining stat dependence is (stat/def)^0.5, which is why a Boon is still worth a few percent rather than nothing.

The action cycle counts ATB plus cast only. Animation and action lockout are not on either of those clocks, so at Instant ATB with a near-zero cast time the throughput figure is optimistic — Fast Act cannot speed up something the cast bar does not govern.
